## Analytical distinction: falsification vs discreditation

| Distinct action | What it would mean | What evidence is necessary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Test mathematical validity | Check deductions, internal consistency, probability calculations and algorithms for errors. | Access to the model and testable derivations. |
| Test the model's assumptions | Check whether starting premises, populations, incentives and underlying data are defensible. | Explicit assumptions and empirical evidence. |
| Evaluate predictions | Compare with observations when possible, assess uncertainty, ranges and calibration; future events cannot yet be observed. | Quantified forecasts, timelines and relevant observable results. |
| Politically discredit the forecaster | Undermine trust, constrain debate, or obtain an authoritative public rebuttal regardless of methodological merit. | Independent evidence of a predetermined political objective. |

**Critical distinction:** Solving a difficult mathematical problem is evidence of extraordinary ability, **not proof** that a completely different predictive model is correct or incorrect. A genuine mathematical critic must be free to find either result.

## Why this matters for the imperial research

A system built on claims of permanent continuity may face a dilemma: it wants reliable warning about future instability while also fearing the political consequences of a credible warning. If critical inquiry is punished or pre-scripted, the regime may weaken its capacity for self-correction. That feedback-loop theory is **conditional**, not a plot prediction.
